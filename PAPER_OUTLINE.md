# Paper Outline and Formatting Guide

This guide describes how to write and format a paper in the style of the supplied *Scholars Journal of Engineering and Technology* sample. Use it as a reusable drafting checklist. Always follow the target journal's latest official author guidelines if they differ from this document.

## 1. Document Setup

- Use A4 paper in portrait orientation.
- Use margins of approximately 18-20 mm on all sides.
- Use a serif typeface similar to Times New Roman.
- Use approximately 9-10 pt type for body text.
- Use approximately 7-8 pt type for running headers, footers, copyright text, and citation details.
- Justify body paragraphs.
- Use single line spacing or similarly compact journal spacing.
- Indent the first line of normal body paragraphs.
- Keep paragraph spacing small and consistent.
- Format the main article in two columns with a narrow gutter.
- Let large tables, diagrams, or figures span both columns when necessary for readability.
- Use dark navy blue for the article title and prominent heading bars.
- Use thin red rules to separate article metadata when reproducing the sample's visual treatment.
- Use thin olive-green borders for citation and footer boxes when reproducing the sample's visual treatment.
- Do not insert decorative elements that do not communicate information.

## 2. Page-by-Page Layout

### Page 1

Place the following elements in this order:

1. Journal masthead
2. Article title
3. Author names
4. Author affiliations
5. DOI and publication-history dates
6. Corresponding-author information
7. Abstract and article-type panel
8. Keywords
9. Copyright or open-access statement
10. Beginning of the two-column article body
11. Citation box and page number

### Page 2 and Later Body Pages

- Place a running header at the top.
- Continue the main text in two columns.
- Keep headings with the text that follows them.
- Place figures close to the paragraph that first discusses them.
- Put the copyright line and page number in the footer.

### Final Page

The final page may contain:

- Remaining body content
- A full-width table or figure
- Acknowledgments
- Disclosure or conflict-of-interest statement
- References and resources
- Copyright footer and page number

Avoid leaving a heading, table title, or figure caption by itself at the bottom of a page.

## 3. Front Matter

### Journal Masthead

The journal normally supplies this material. If preparing an internal draft, use placeholders for:

- Full journal name
- Abbreviated journal title
- Print ISSN
- Online ISSN
- Journal homepage
- Open-access label or mark

Do not invent journal identifiers or imply acceptance by a journal.

### Article Title

- State the central problem, proposed contribution, and application clearly.
- Prefer a specific title over a broad or promotional one.
- Use title case consistently.
- Keep the title concise where possible.
- Avoid unexplained abbreviations.
- Do not end the title with a period.
- Format the title in dark blue at approximately 16-18 pt.

Suggested pattern:

> [Method or System] for [Problem or Outcome] in [Application Area]

### Authors and Affiliations

- List authors in the agreed authorship order.
- Add superscript numbers when authors have different affiliations.
- Add an asterisk to the corresponding author's name.
- Give each author's department or role, organization, city, state or region, postal code, and country as required by the journal.
- Confirm the spelling of every name and organization.
- Do not list someone as an author without their approval.

Example:

```text
First Author1*, Second Author2
1Role or Department, Organization, City, State, Postal Code, Country
2Role or Department, Organization, City, State, Postal Code, Country
```

### Publication Metadata

Provide labeled placeholders for:

```text
DOI: [assigned by journal]
Received: [DD.MM.YYYY] | Accepted: [DD.MM.YYYY] | Published: [DD.MM.YYYY]
```

- Never create a DOI yourself.
- Do not retain sample values such as `00X`, `N/A`, or another paper's dates.
- Leave journal-controlled fields blank or mark them clearly as journal supplied.

### Corresponding Author

Include:

- Full name
- Professional title or role
- Organization and address
- Email address, if required

## 4. Abstract

- Write the abstract as one self-contained paragraph.
- Aim for approximately 150-250 words unless the journal specifies another limit.
- Do not use citations, footnotes, tables, figures, or undefined abbreviations.
- Use past tense for work performed and present tense for conclusions or general facts.
- Avoid unsupported promotional claims such as "revolutionary" or "best."
- Make every claim in the abstract consistent with the paper's body.

Write the abstract in this sequence:

1. **Context:** What field or operating environment does the paper address?
2. **Problem:** What specific limitation or risk exists?
3. **Gap:** Why are current methods insufficient?
4. **Approach:** What system, method, model, or framework is proposed or studied?
5. **Evidence or operation:** What data, process, experiment, or evaluation supports it?
6. **Result:** What was found or produced?
7. **Significance:** Why does the result matter in practice?

Template:

```text
[Context and problem.] Existing approaches are limited by [specific gap].
This paper presents [method, system, or framework] that [main function].
The approach uses [data, process, or evaluation method]. [Main result or
demonstrated capability.] These findings indicate that [practical or technical
significance].
```

### Article Type

Place the journal's article classification on the right side of the abstract header bar, for example:

- Research Article
- Review Article
- Technical Article
- Case Study

Choose the type based on the actual paper. Do not label original, unvalidated work as a review article merely to match the sample.

## 5. Keywords

- Provide approximately four to eight keywords unless the journal specifies otherwise.
- Separate keywords with semicolons.
- Include the core method, subject, application area, and important technical terms.
- Prefer searchable terms used by researchers in the field.
- Avoid repeating every word from the title.
- Define abbreviations in the paper even if they appear as keywords.
- Never leave `Keywords: Missing` in the submitted manuscript.

Example:

```text
Keywords: business risk assessment; real-time indicators; credit scoring;
supply-chain operations; decision models
```

## 6. Main Paper Structure

Use section titles that match the nature of the work. The sample uses an invention-oriented structure, but a research paper may require Methods, Results, and Discussion instead.

### 6.1 Field of Invention or Field of Study

Explain:

- The technical or professional field
- The class of problem being addressed
- The users, organizations, or systems affected
- The intended application of the work

Keep this section focused. Do not present the full solution or repeat the abstract.

### 6.2 Background of Invention or Background

Explain:

- Current practices and established approaches
- The limitations of those approaches
- The operational, technical, or research consequences
- Prior work relevant to the problem
- The unresolved gap addressed by the paper

Support factual claims with credible citations. Distinguish measured facts from assumptions and professional observations.

End the background with a clear gap statement, such as:

> However, existing approaches do not provide [missing capability], particularly when [important condition].

### 6.3 Objective or Contribution

State exactly what the paper contributes. Depending on the paper, this may be:

- A new system or product architecture
- A model or algorithm
- A structured framework
- An empirical finding
- A comparison or evaluation
- A practical implementation

Use precise verbs such as *develops*, *evaluates*, *compares*, *measures*, or *demonstrates*.

### 6.4 Description of Invention, Proposed System, or Methodology

Describe the work so that a qualified reader can understand and, when appropriate, reproduce it.

Cover:

1. Inputs or source data
2. Main components
3. Processing steps or workflow
4. Decision logic, model, or equations
5. Outputs
6. Validation or testing method
7. Assumptions and constraints
8. Privacy, security, ethical, or regulatory considerations where relevant

Use bullets only for genuinely parallel items. Use numbered steps for processes that must occur in order.

### 6.5 Results or Demonstrated Outcomes

If the paper reports research or testing, include a dedicated Results section.

- State the sample size, data period, and evaluation conditions.
- Define all metrics before reporting them.
- Report numerical results with units.
- Include uncertainty, error, confidence intervals, or limitations where relevant.
- Do not claim effectiveness without evidence.
- Do not mix interpretation into every result; reserve broader meaning for the Discussion section.

### 6.6 Discussion

Explain:

- What the results mean
- Why they matter
- How they compare with prior work
- Where the approach performs well or poorly
- What tradeoffs and risks remain
- Whether the results generalize beyond the tested setting

### 6.7 Limitations

State material limitations openly, including:

- Data coverage or quality limitations
- Untested scenarios
- Model assumptions
- Potential bias
- Implementation constraints
- Dependence on third-party systems

### 6.8 Conclusion

- Restate the problem and principal contribution briefly.
- Summarize the most important supported finding.
- Explain the practical implication.
- Identify realistic future work if needed.
- Do not introduce new evidence, citations, or claims.

## 7. Paragraph and Heading Rules

- Use a consistent heading hierarchy throughout the paper.
- Do not change capitalization styles between sections.
- Keep headings short and descriptive.
- Begin each paragraph with one clear controlling idea.
- Follow the topic sentence with evidence, explanation, or an example.
- Avoid paragraphs that contain only one sentence unless emphasis requires it.
- Define each acronym at first use: `business credibility product (BCP)`.
- Use the same term for the same concept throughout the paper.
- Prefer direct, formal language.
- Remove conversational fillers, repetition, and unsupported adjectives.
- Use active voice when the actor matters and passive voice when the process matters more.

## 8. Figures and Diagrams

- Introduce every figure in the body before it appears.
- Number figures in order: Figure 1, Figure 2, and so on.
- Give every figure a descriptive caption.
- Make labels readable at final printed size.
- Use consistent colors, shapes, line weights, and typography.
- Explain all abbreviations and symbols in the caption or a legend.
- Preserve sufficient contrast for grayscale printing and accessibility.
- Do not stretch, blur, or pixelate images.
- Cite the source and obtain permission for material not created by the authors.
- Refer to the figure by number, not as "the figure below."

Example:

```text
Figure 1: Data sources and decision flow of the proposed risk-assessment system.
```

## 9. Tables

- Introduce every table in the text before it appears.
- Number tables in order: Table 1, Table 2, and so on.
- Place the table title above the table unless the journal requires another style.
- Use clear column headings with measurement units.
- Keep terminology and capitalization consistent.
- Align numbers by decimal point where practical.
- Explain abbreviations and special symbols in table notes.
- Do not duplicate the same information in both a table and a figure.
- Split a table only when it cannot fit legibly on one page.
- Repeat header rows when a table continues onto another page.

The sample's category table uses:

- A full-width group heading
- A narrow left column for the category
- A wider right column for numbered variables or parameters
- Thin black borders
- Compact row spacing

## 10. Acknowledgments

- Include this section only when needed.
- Thank contributors who assisted but do not meet authorship criteria.
- Identify funding, institutional, technical, or editorial support as required.
- Obtain permission before naming a person in the acknowledgments.
- Do not use acknowledgments for endorsements or promotional statements.

## 11. Disclosure and Conflicts of Interest

State:

- Relevant employment or organizational relationships
- Financial interests
- Funding sources
- Intellectual-property interests
- Whether the work represents personal views or an organization's official position

If no conflict exists, use the target journal's preferred no-conflict wording.

## 12. References and Resources

- Cite every external factual claim that requires support.
- Use primary and authoritative sources where possible.
- Verify that every in-text citation appears in the reference list.
- Verify that every listed reference is cited in the text.
- Use one citation style consistently; replace the sample's bullet format if the target journal requires numbered or author-date references.
- Include complete bibliographic information.
- Include DOI links when available and required.
- Do not invent sources, authors, titles, publication details, or DOIs.
- Confirm that links work and that titles and author names are spelled correctly.

Generic author-date example:

```text
Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of article. Journal Name,
volume(issue), page-page. https://doi.org/xxxxx
```

## 13. Running Header, Citation Box, and Footer

### Running Header

On pages after the first, use a compact line containing:

```text
[Author surname(s)], [Journal abbreviation], [Month, Year]; [Volume(Issue)]: [Pages]
```

The journal normally finalizes this information.

### Citation Box

At the bottom of page 1, include the journal's preferred citation format and the page number. Do not guess final volume, issue, DOI, or page numbers.

### Footer

Use a thin bordered footer containing:

- Copyright year
- Journal or publisher name
- Publisher information if required
- Page number aligned to the right

## 15. Final Quality Checklist

### Content

- [ ] The title accurately reflects the paper.
- [ ] The abstract contains the problem, approach, evidence, result, and significance.
- [ ] Keywords are present and searchable.
- [ ] The research gap is explicit.
- [ ] The contribution is specific and supported.
- [ ] Methods are clear enough to understand or reproduce.
- [ ] Results include appropriate evidence and units.
- [ ] Claims do not exceed the evidence.
- [ ] Limitations are stated.
- [ ] The conclusion introduces no new claims.

### Accuracy and Integrity

- [ ] Every author approved the final manuscript and authorship order.
- [ ] Affiliations and corresponding-author details are correct.
- [ ] No DOI, journal date, volume, issue, or page number was invented.
- [ ] All facts, quotations, data, figures, and borrowed ideas are cited.
- [ ] No reference was fabricated.
- [ ] Conflicts, funding, and organizational relationships are disclosed.
- [ ] Confidential or personally identifiable information has been removed or authorized.

### Formatting

- [ ] The document uses A4 portrait pages.
- [ ] Fonts, sizes, margins, colors, and spacing are consistent.
- [ ] The main body uses a balanced two-column layout.
- [ ] Headings follow one hierarchy and capitalization style.
- [ ] Paragraphs are justified and consistently indented.
- [ ] Each figure and table is numbered, titled, cited, and legible.
- [ ] Running headers, footers, and page numbers are consistent.
- [ ] No heading, caption, or table title is stranded at a page break.
- [ ] There are no clipped lines, overlapping elements, blank placeholders, or low-resolution graphics.

### Final Submission

- [ ] Journal-controlled fields are blank or clearly marked as journal supplied.
- [ ] Placeholder text such as `Missing`, `N/A`, `00X`, and `[DATE]` has been resolved.
- [ ] Spelling, grammar, punctuation, terminology, and acronym use have been checked.
- [ ] In-text citations and the reference list match.
- [ ] The final PDF has been inspected page by page at normal size and at 100% zoom.
- [ ] The manuscript complies with the target journal's current official author instructions.
